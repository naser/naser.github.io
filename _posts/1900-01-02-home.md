THREADS Lab is the research group of [Naser Ezzati-Jivan]({{ site.baseurl }}/tabs/bio/), Associate Professor in the [Department of Computer Science at Brock University](https://brocku.ca/mathematics-science/computer-science/faculty-staff/naser-ezzati-jivan/). We study how software, hardware, and AI systems actually behave — and build the tracing, observability, and analysis methods needed to explain, trust, and improve them.

### Research directions

**A system can only be understood, trusted, or improved to the extent that its runtime behaviour can be observed as evidence** — so we build the tracing and analysis methods that turn what a system actually does at runtime into evidence you can reason about, whether that system is a distributed service, an AI agent, or a robot.

Our work sits in three connected areas. See the [Research page]({{ site.baseurl }}/tabs/research/) for the full detail, current projects, and team.

**1. Software observability, tracing & performance engineering**\
Adaptive, multilevel tracing and root-cause methods spanning application code down to kernel and hardware evidence.
- Multi-Level Adaptive Tracing for Enhancing Software Reliability and Performance — Mitacs Business Strategy Internship, with Ciena
- Observability Compensation Paradigm: Leveraging Adaptive Execution Tracing and Analysis — NSERC Discovery Grant

**2. Trustworthy & agentic AI systems**\
Making AI-driven and agentic software systems observable, auditable, and fair.
- EDI-by-Design in the Age of AI — NSERC Dimensions Canada / SSHRC Insight Development / Match of Minds
- RapidBOM: Multimodal Document AI and Privacy-Preserving Knowledge Graphs for Engineering Sourcing — Mitacs Accelerate
- Intelligent Software Agents for Autonomous Monitoring and Repair — Mitacs Globalink
- Harnessing Large Language Models for Automated Software Log Understanding — Mitacs Globalink

**3. Heterogeneous execution & embodied/physical systems**\
Extending observability beyond conventional software boundaries into heterogeneous hardware and the physical world.
- Observability and benchmarking for AI inference on heterogeneous hardware — ongoing project
- Observability and energy-usage analysis for physical AI platforms — robots, drones, and other embodied systems

### News and recent work

The latest announcements cover software performance engineering, distributed and kernel tracing, observability, and the governance of agentic AI systems.

- **Mitacs Globalink interns join the lab (2026).** Eman Chaudhary, Khaled Gardabbou, and Muhammad Maaz Hamid begin fully funded internships on automated log understanding and autonomous agent monitoring.
- **IVADO Visiting Scholar grant (2026).** Scholarly visit to Polytechnique Montréal, hosted by Prof. Foutse Khomh.
- **New research assistants join the lab (2026).** Flora Nah Asah, Mahsa Jafaie Menarbazari, Abhijeet Dhali, and Becky Kwok begin work on EDI-by-Design and Mitacs-partnered projects.
- **NSERC Dimensions Canada and SSHRC Insight Development grants awarded (2026),** supporting *EDI-by-Design in the Age of AI* and related trustworthy-AI research.
- **Mitacs Accelerate award for RapidBOM (2026)** — a new industry-partnered project on multimodal document AI and privacy-preserving knowledge graphs.
- **CASCON 2026 — paper accepted.** Our paper, **Beyond Agent Logs: Cross-Layer Execution Evidence for Auditing Coding Agents**, by Haitham Al Satai, Naser Ezzati-Jivan, and Foutse Khomh, has been accepted for the [CASCON 2026 technical-paper program](https://conf.researchr.org/track/cascon-2026/cascontechnical-papers2026). It examines cross-layer execution evidence for auditing coding-agent behavior.
- **ACM FSE 2026 — agentic-AI governance briefing.** [Taming the Stochastic Machine: Observability and Runtime Governance for Agentic AI Systems](https://conf.researchr.org/track/fse-2026/fse-2026-tutorials), co-presented with Maryam Ekhlasi. The official program lists this as a 90-minute technical briefing on observability and runtime governance for agentic AI systems.
- **DTraComp — Journal of Systems and Software.** [DTraComp: Comparing distributed execution traces for understanding intermittent latency sources](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2026.112990) presents an open-source framework for comparing distributed execution traces and connecting request-level latency to thread-level kernel evidence.
- **LMAT — Journal of Systems and Software.** [LMAT: An adaptive tracing approach based on efficient system behavior analysis using language models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2026.112890) introduces adaptive tracing that uses language models to tune tracing granularity from observed system behavior.
- **CARE — IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering.** [CARE: Context Aware Root Cause Identification Using Distributed Traces and Profiling Metrics](https://naser.github.io/research-publications/papers/care-context-aware-root-cause-identification/), by Mahsa Panahandeh, Naser Ezzati-Jivan, Abdelwahab Hamou-Lhadj, and James Miller, integrates distributed traces, profiling metrics, and network analysis for context-aware root-cause identification. The paper was also featured in the [ACM FSE 2026 Journal-First program](https://conf.researchr.org/track/fse-2026/fse-2026-journal-first).
